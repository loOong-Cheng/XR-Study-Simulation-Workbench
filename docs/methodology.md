# HCI methodological decisions

## Two questions, two interventions

Standardized exposure asks how agents predict after a defined set of experiences. Autonomous exploration asks how well a complete agent workflow chooses and uses evidence. Access to a tool, recorded exposure and effective use of its information are distinct. Do not interpret a bundled workflow comparison as the isolated contribution of stereoscopy, reasoning effort or demographics.

Required case ordering is explicit. Individual case packets follow the participant's assigned design order, with phases ordered within each design. Population packets describe the counterbalance rather than implying every design is first. Relative scoring removes common scale/offset differences but does not remove differential learning, fatigue or carryover.

The planner's defaults are design proposals, not HCI best-practice mandates. Researchers determine meaningful task coverage, predictor timing, interaction policies and measurement windows. An arbitrary minimum number of screenshots is not evidence of meaningful exploration.

## Reference reliability

The independent human sample size is the number of people, not the number of predicted values, design pairs or screenshots. Preserve participant-level measurements for uncertainty. Report informative counts for each outcome and assess human retest or split-half reliability separately where justified. This prototype does not create a universal score threshold or a human reliability ceiling.

Tie policy is numerical, not a claim about practical equivalence. The configured tolerance (`tieTolerance`, default 1e-8) handles floating-point ties: two design values whose difference is within it are tied, and before ranking, every rank test rounds its values to multiples of it, so values that differ only by floating-point noise (|1/3 − 0.5| and |2/3 − 0.5| differ by 6e-17) share a rank, and a paired difference that rounds to zero is dropped. Where no study is at hand (agreement scores in the figures), values are rounded at 1e-9, relative to the largest absolute value when that exceeds 1. A practically meaningful indifference region would need a separately justified and frozen measurement-specific rule. Human-tied pairs provide no direction and are excluded; forecast ties carry no directional information and receive half credit.

Individual agreement with common design preferences is not proof of correct individual matching. A future identity-permutation analysis can test that question; it is not implemented here. Population and individual scores are different estimands and must not be ranked together.

## Grouping and composites

Declare outcomes, groups and weights using the research question and measurement theory. Do not count a questionnaire total and each of its component items as independent evidence. Keep distinct preference questions identifiable, even when their family budget is shared. Group overlapping navigation or time-segment measures rather than giving repeated entries extra influence.

This framework uses predeclared families, not correlations selected for favorable simulator performance. If groups are derived from human GT, disclose that choice and its uncertainty, and keep it out of agent inputs. The reported bootstrap conditions on the chosen grouping.

For the example's modified workload measure, Performance is omitted explicitly and the five retained item means are recomputed for both humans and forecasts. Scaling a six-item total preserves its ordering but does not remove the Performance contribution. Ambiguous or reversed questionnaire responses require researcher evidence, not automatic score-driven correction.

A composite is computed from its items by the rule declared with the measure: the item mean (the default, as before), or the sum, each optionally weighted (`componentRule`, `componentWeights`; see the data contract). The questionnaire's own scoring decides: the SSQ total is a weighted sum (3.74 × the raw subscale sums), so a correct total was refused while only means were possible. Human records and forecasts are checked against the same rule. Only the composite is scored; its items are not scored separately. A subscale that matters for the research question is declared as its own measure, in the composite's family so that the family's weight is not counted twice.

## Timing, workload and sickness

Human completion time includes perception, action and decision processes that are not represented by a model's network latency. A simulator clock may also advance differently from wall time. Report predicted human time separately from measured agent/environment execution.

Head and hand motion can be informative, but raw motion during an experimenter's handling of an unworn headset is not participant task behavior. Preserve wear/phase markers and exclude invalid windows when preparing task summaries. Subjective exposure may include practice and earlier blocks; model those inputs and carryover explicitly without leaking the target rating. Sickness prediction is not validated merely by rendering stereo frames or matching a population sickness rank.

## Validity and reporting

Plan hashes bind content, not history. A plan frozen after looking at outcomes remains post hoc even if it has a digest. Use an independently timestamped archive or registration for prospective claims. Changes create a new version, and confirmatory interpretation must follow the actual order of events.

Report all assigned jobs and deviations. The audit runs every check on every returned run and lists every deviation it finds; one failed check (for example the token budget) no longer hides the others. Before this, the first failed compliance check stopped the audit, so a run over its token budget showed only that line even when required tasks were never observed. Which runs are compliant does not change, only what is listed. The token budget covers the tokens used until the forecast was accepted when the execution record holds that count (`tokensAtSubmit`); the session's closing turn after the forecast is then named separately and not counted. Without it, the session's total is compared. Completion-only and adherence-only subsets can select different workflows or participants; label those analyses as sensitivities. Technical failures and an incorrect forecast are different outcomes. Missing outputs are not automatically worse-than-chance predictions, but differential failure rates can invalidate a comparison.

Intervals and centered-bootstrap contrasts are exploratory approximations for a discrete ranking statistic. Small samples, near ties, singleton strata and deterministic forecasts can undermine them. There is no p-value for empirically degenerate variance. The Holm family for comparisons between configurations is the set of comparisons chosen at analysis (`comparisons.json`, or those stored with the plan when none are chosen); the report labels each one as stored in the plan or chosen at analysis. Choose that family before inspecting results; if it was chosen afterwards, report the comparisons as exploratory. Nonsignificance does not establish equivalence, absence of benefit or demographic harm.

Real and simulated runs are never pooled. When a configuration has both, its simulated runs are left out of every score and figure, and stay listed in the audit ("1 real + 17 simulated; simulated left out"). A configuration with only simulated runs is scored on them and named in a note, because a comparison with a configuration scored on real runs then compares invented with real forecasts. Simulated runs are only scoreable in synthetic studies.

Invented forecasts are therefore never *tested* against real ones. Every figure marks a configuration with only simulated runs: its label ends in "(simulated)", it is drawn dashed and hollow, and a legend line and the figure's note say so (the report lists these configurations in `simulatedConfigs`). Brackets join only configurations of the same kind. The brackets between configurations with real runs form the figure's Holm family; brackets between two simulated configurations form a separate family and are drawn dashed (they test the pipeline). Simulated configurations get no test against chance (agreement) and none against the human data (agent vs human: their discrepancy is shown with "Not tested", outside the Holm family). A planned comparison between a simulated and a real configuration shows its difference without an interval or p-value ("Not tested: compares simulated (invented) with real runs"); it still counts in the declared Holm family, like every untestable comparison. Before this, figures drew no difference, and a tester saw a Holm bracket (p = .026) between a real and a simulated configuration.

The same holds across campaigns and continuations. Comparing two campaigns names the kind of runs behind each score, and a configuration scored on real runs in one campaign and on simulated runs in the other is marked not comparable, without a change value. A continuation made on the website reuses only sessions with an accepted forecast, and by default treats simulated sessions like sessions that never ran, so real agents replace them; reusing them is a choice the researcher makes for trying the pipeline.

Runtime provenance comes from the runtime. Each app session's runtime reports its engine and version, backend, device profile, observation and capabilities in the trace; the audit compares that report with the frozen recipe. Before this, the execution record copied these fields from the study, so the check could not fail. Earlier real runs without such a report are now listed with the deviation "the runtime did not report itself". Their scores do not change, but they drop out of the adherence-only sensitivity analysis.

How the app was played is audited too, not only whether each task was seen once. These checks only add deviations; scoreability and the scores do not change.

- *Least actions.* A task's `minActions` (for example one answer per trial) is checked: fewer scored actions in the task is a deviation. Scored actions are task answers (a selection, a click, an app action the app declares as scored). The WebXR runtime marks them (`scored` on each action event; looking, pointing and the runtime's own `complete-task` are not answers, and an action the app refused is neither completed nor scored). For a runtime that marks none, every completed action except `complete-task` counts. Before this, `minActions` only sized the tool budget.
- *A view before each action.* A task may require an observation before each scored action (`observeBeforeEachAction: true` in `study.json`). An answer without a screenshot in the task since the previous answer is then a deviation. This targets agents that read the task order from the app's code and click from memory: in a tester's pointing study, agents read `main.js`, learned the target order and clicked about 350 ms apart without looking.
- *Unfinished tasks.* Opening the next design before the app reported the task done, or ending the session, records the task as not submitted (before, leaving a design marked it submitted).
- *Reading the project.* A configuration that reads the project (the read-files tool with a project source) gets only the project's file list in its prompt, which the trace records as delivered. A session that never opens a project file is a deviation ("did not open any project file"); before, two such sessions with no tool calls were recorded as compliant.
- *Simulated runs* invent their actions and mark them as not checked against a runtime (`checked: false`), so a required action the app does not offer shows up only in real runs. `study-check` warns when a WebXR task requires an action that the runtime never records for the declared app.

**Task logic the agent can read.** An app configuration that also reads the project can learn the task's answers (the target order, which object is right) from the code instead of the screen. Give app configurations no project files, or keep the task's order and answers in data the agent cannot read (for example generated from the participant seed at run time, or in a file outside the project folder), and consider `observeBeforeEachAction` for answer-by-answer tasks. Either way, report which configurations could read the app.

The WebXR app is frozen with the campaign: its folder (every file, in a fixed order, OS clutter left out), its URL query and what `workspace.json` declares about it (capabilities, actions, instructions) are fingerprinted in `sources.json` at freeze and continuation, and app sessions are refused when any of them changed, like changed knowledge. A continuation then runs the configurations that play the app again, as it does for configurations that read a changed source. Engine project folders are not fingerprinted, since engines rewrite them when opened. Folder fingerprints map paths to `/` before sorting by bytes, so a folder frozen on Windows reads as unchanged on macOS and Linux; `desktop.ini`, `Thumbs.db`, `.DS_Store`, Office lock files (`~$…`) and other dot files are left out. Folders that hold such files, or that were frozen on Windows with nested paths, can read as changed once after this change; freeze again or continue.

**Within- and between-subjects studies.** Which human design differences agents are judged on depends on the design.

- *Between-subjects:* each person experienced one design, so the designs are compared as independent groups. A difference counts only when a test supports it: Mann–Whitney U between the two groups for every pair of designs, Holm-adjusted within each measure, α = .05. Agents are scored on those pairs only; the others are listed with their p-values and not scored, and a measure without any such pair is excluded for every configuration. Small groups can therefore leave little or nothing to score (with two people per group, no pair can reach significance). The Study page and `study-check` warn before anything is frozen when no pair can: the smallest exact two-sided Mann–Whitney p is 2 / C(n1 + n2, n1) (complete separation), and Holm multiplies the smallest p of a measure by its number of design pairs, so nothing can be significant when that product is at least .05. The same check runs within subjects with the smallest exact Wilcoxon p, 2 / 2^n for n people (3 people: .25, so the significant-only view would be empty; with 3 designs at least 7 people are needed). Missing values or tied differences can only raise these p-values, so the warning is never too strict. Preference rankings are refused (nobody saw more than one design), and so are individual configurations (one design per person leaves nothing to order). The bootstrap resamples whole people within their group.
- *Within-subjects:* the main scores keep every difference of the design means, as before, so earlier analyses stay comparable. A second view scores the population configurations on significant paired differences only (Wilcoxon signed-rank on each person's difference, the log ratio for geometric measures, Holm within each measure, α = .05). Individual configurations have no such test, since one person's profile is a single observation per design.
- *Absolute values:* for every population configuration, measure and design, the analyzer reports whether the mean of the runs' predictions lies inside the human group's 95% t interval (geometric measures on the log scale), and the signed error. Intervals of proportions are clipped to [0, 1], those of mean ranks to [1, number of designs] and those of counts at 0; a declared `range` (for example 1–7) clips them too. Measures without bounds are left as computed. Before ranges could be declared, a rating's interval could reach past the scale's end (for example above 7), and a count's below 0 (a tester saw −1.10 for collisions). Clipping changes only the reported interval: "inside" compares the mean forecast with the unclipped interval, which gives the same answer because a forecast cannot leave the range. When every person gave the same value, the interval has zero width; the row is marked as degenerate ("all n people gave the same value"), and "inside" then means an exact match within the tie tolerance. In a between-subjects study these group values (completion time, workload, aesthetics) are what the study compares.

**Tests, p-values and intervals.**

- *Exact and approximate p-values.* Mann–Whitney U and Wilcoxon signed-rank give exact conditional (permutation) p-values whenever the sample is small enough, also with ties: the null distribution of the rank sum (Mann–Whitney) or of the sum of positive ranks (Wilcoxon) given the observed midranks, computed by dynamic programming over midranks doubled to integers. Wilcoxon drops differences that are zero after rounding to the tie tolerance, as before. The cutoff is set by computing cost: exact up to n1 + n2 = 80 for Mann–Whitney (about 5 ms per test) and up to 200 non-zero differences for Wilcoxon (about 4 ms); above it, the normal approximation with tie and continuity correction. Two-sided p = min(1, 2 × the smaller one-sided tail probability), each tail including the observed value. With ties, the conditional distribution of the Mann–Whitney rank sum can be asymmetric; then this convention can differ from counting rank sums at least as far from the mean (for example [1,2,3,3,4,5,5,5] vs [3,4,5,6,6,7]: .083 against .074). Previously, ties always switched to the normal approximation, which is anti-conservative in small samples ([1,1,1] vs [2,2,2]: .047 instead of the exact .100; with four differences it gave .07–.10, although the smallest exact p is .125). Every value tied (Mann–Whitney) or fewer than two non-zero differences (Wilcoxon) gives no test. The values agree with a full permutation enumeration in R.
- *Geometric measures.* Every paired test of a geometric measure runs on the log scale (each person's log ratio): the analyzer's human pairs, the human-data figure and the agent-vs-human figure. Unpaired rank tests do not depend on the scale, but use the same log values so that rounding of near-ties is identical.
- *Holm.* One function adjusts every family (design pairs of a measure, figure brackets, comparisons between configurations). Untestable comparisons (no p-value) stay untested but count in the family size, which is conservative: the family is what was declared, not what happened to be testable.
- *Which configurations a figure shows, and so tests.* Figures follow the scores. An individual configuration is left out until every participant has a primary forecast (it is not scored before), and a configuration is left out of a measure (agent vs human) or a domain (agreement, population vs individual) it does not predict, for example a measure it is given as an input; a note under the figure names what was left out and why. Left-out configurations get no row and no bracket, so they never enter the figure's Holm family. Among the configurations shown, a comparison that cannot be tested (for example a configuration without forecasts yet) still counts in the family size.
- *t quantile.* The 95% t intervals use the exact 0.975 quantile of Student's t for any degrees of freedom (inverse of the t distribution function via the regularized incomplete beta function), equal to R's `qt(0.975, df)` to 1e-12. A table with an approximation above 30 degrees of freedom was used before; it was off by up to 0.03 above 60.
- *Agent vs human figure.* Individual-level configurations forecast each participant, so the test is a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank on (forecast − that person's value). Population runs are not people: the test is a one-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank of the human values against the configuration's mean forecast (H0: the human values are centred on the forecast), which treats the forecast as fixed. Both on the log scale for geometric measures; each row names its test; Holm across the figure's table.
- *Figure brackets condition on the fixed human sample.* Agreement scores in the figures are computed against the one human sample that exists; brackets between configurations and tests against 50% compare those scores and do not include the sampling uncertainty of the human data. The bootstrap intervals in the score table do (people are resampled).
- *Population vs individual figure.* A population run's value is its per-run score from the analyzer (same pair masks, same handling of measures without a scored pair, same weights); individual configurations are averaged to the cohort per repetition and scored the same way. In a between-subjects study this means significant group differences only.
- *Bootstrap streams.* People are resampled from one stream shared by every configuration (so comparisons use paired draws); each configuration resamples its runs from its own stream, seeded from the study seed and the configuration's ID. Results stay reproducible, and one configuration's interval no longer changes when another configuration is added, removed or reordered (before, all shared one stream).

**Comparing configurations without human data.** Run-to-run agreement and agreement between configurations use the same directional idea as the human comparison, but between forecasts: for every pair of designs, two forecasts that order them the same way score 1, opposite 0, a tie on one side 0.5, and a tie on both sides 1 (two forecasts that both see no difference agree; against humans, tied human pairs are left out instead). A tie is a difference within the study's tie tolerance. Scores are averaged over design pairs and then over the outcomes both forecasts cover, per domain, with the study's family and outcome weights exactly as in the analyzer's scores. Population runs are compared pairwise; individual runs only with runs for the same person. Between configurations, each configuration's average forecast is used (individual ones averaged over people). These describe consistency and similarity, not accuracy, and carry no test.

An analysis made before every session has finished is interim. The analyzer says how many sessions finished, failed or were never run, does not count never-run sessions as lost runs, and marks saved figures as interim.

Select protocol variants on synthetic or development tasks, then freeze before held-out evaluation. An LLM can help propose methods and explain results; deterministic code calculates the reported scores. Do not optimize agent designs repeatedly against the same hidden human targets and call the resulting evaluation independent.
