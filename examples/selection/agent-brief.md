# Study brief (given to every agent)

You are forecasting how a group of people will perform and feel when they pick up and place objects in virtual reality with three selection techniques. You have not seen their answers.

**The room.** A plain, evenly lit room, about 6 m × 5 m. The participant starts standing just in front of a long standing-height table (2.4 m wide, 1 m deep, 0.9 m high). Ten small objects (about 9 cm) stand on the table in two rows, one near the participant's side and one along the far side. They are cubes, cylinders and spheres in red, blue and yellow. Several are look-alikes that share shape and colour and differ only by a thin white band. Two more objects stand on a shelf by the left wall, about 3 m away. To the right, about 1.5 m from the start, a small side table carries the green goal zone (a 30 cm square).

**The task.** In every task one object glows, and a panel in front of the participant says: "Pick up the glowing object and put it in the green zone." The task ends when the object rests in the zone. Picking up another object counts as a wrong selection; the participant puts it back and continues.

**The three techniques** (the room, the objects and the tasks stay the same):

- **Ray selection:** a laser ray from the right controller. Point at an object and press the trigger to pick it up; it follows the ray. Point at the zone and release to put it there. Works at any distance the ray can see.
- **Direct touch:** reach out with the right hand and grab the object. Only objects within arm's reach can be grabbed, so far objects need walking or teleporting first. Carry the object to the zone and let go inside it.
- **Hybrid selection:** direct touch for objects within arm's reach, and a ray for objects beyond it. The switch happens automatically.

**Moving around.** Participants can walk inside a 2 m × 2 m play area, and teleport with the controller's thumbstick: an arc shows the landing spot, up to about 4 m away. Each completed teleport is counted.

**The procedure.** 12 adults each use all three techniques, in a counterbalanced order (each of the six possible orders is used by two people). With each technique they:

1. do one guided practice task (a nearby object), then
2. do six scored tasks: two objects in the near row, two in the far row of the table, two on the shelf;
3. answer a short questionnaire, and
4. take a short break.

After all three techniques they rank them twice: which they prefer to interact with, and which they prefer overall.

**What to predict for each technique** (population averages):

| Outcome | How it is measured |
| --- | --- |
| Completion time | Seconds from the object starting to glow to the object resting in the zone (geometric mean over the six scored tasks) |
| Selection accuracy | Correct pickups / all pickups in the scored tasks (proportion) |
| Teleport count | Completed teleports per scored task |
| Workload | Mean of five 1–7 items: mental, physical, temporal, effort, frustration (no Performance item) |
| Visual appeal | 1–7 |
| Interaction preference | Rank, 1 = preferred to interact with |
| Overall preference | Rank, 1 = preferred overall |

Report each value for ray, direct and hybrid, plus your uncertainty and reasoning.
