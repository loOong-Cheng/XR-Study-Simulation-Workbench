# Toy evidence note (invented for this example)

These statements are invented for demonstration. They are not a literature review, they were written without looking at the synthetic participants' answers, and they were not fitted to them. In a real study, put your evidence document here, for example a summary of published findings or a computational model.

- Pointing with a ray reaches far objects without moving. Its precision falls as an object's angular size shrinks with distance, and look-alike neighbours along the same line of sight make wrong picks more likely.
- Grabbing with the hand is precise for objects within reach, but objects beyond arm's reach first need walking or teleporting. Each teleport adds aiming and re-orientation time.
- Techniques that switch between hand and ray can combine the strengths of both. They also add a moment of uncertainty about which mode is active near the switching distance.
- Physical demand tends to follow how much people reach, walk and teleport. Mental demand tends to follow how much precise aiming a task needs.
- Visual appeal depends mostly on the room and objects, which do not change between techniques here. Differences may still appear through the ray's or hand's own visuals.
- Preference rankings tend to follow how efficient and how effortful a technique felt, but individuals differ.
