# Task brief: temperature visualization in a VR building

This is a synthetic example study. The setup follows a real experiment design. The participants and all their answers are invented.

## Setting

Participants wear a head-mounted display and stand in a virtual three-storey office building. The building's simulated indoor air temperature is shown with one of four visualization designs. They move by walking within the play area and by teleporting with the controller.

## The four designs (within subjects, counterbalanced with a Williams square)

| ID | Design | How temperature is shown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SP | Spheres | A regular grid of small colored spheres fills the rooms; color encodes temperature. |
| VO | Volume | A semi-transparent colored volume fills the building; denser color where it is warmer or colder than the mean. |
| PP | Probe Plane | A colored horizontal slice that the participant moves up and down with the controller. |
| HP | Handheld Probe | A handheld wand that shows the temperature at its tip as a number and a color. |

## Tasks per design

1. **Practice:** two place comparisons and one zone question, with feedback. Not scored.
2. **Main task:**
   - **8 place comparisons:** two marked places are shown; the participant decides which one is warmer.
   - **4 zone questions:** the participant finds the warmest zone of a floor (one of four).

   After each trial, the participant rates their confidence from 1 to 5.
3. **Questionnaires** after each design:
   - perceived workload (1–7);
   - BeauVis aesthetic pleasure (1–7);
   - Simulator Sickness Questionnaire (total score 0–235.62).

After all four designs, participants rank them (1 = best) for comparing places, judging zones, interaction, understanding the building, and overall.

## What is measured

- Place and zone accuracy (proportion correct).
- Median time per trial.
- Confidence, workload, BeauVis and SSQ per design.
- The five final rankings.
