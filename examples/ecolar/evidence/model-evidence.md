# Computational model evidence (invented for this example)

The numbers below are **invented**. They are not results of real models and were not fitted to the synthetic participants. They show the kind of table a researcher might give agents: outputs of simple computational user models for each design. In a real study, put your own model outputs here.

| Design | Visual search model: expected fixations to find a marker | Color-discrimination model: just-noticeable difference (K) | Occlusion model: share of the field visible from a typical position | Motor model: controller actions per trial |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SP Spheres | 6.1 | 0.9 | 0.35 | 1.0 |
| VO Volume | 4.2 | 1.6 | 0.80 | 1.0 |
| PP Probe Plane | 5.0 | 0.8 | 0.55 | 3.2 |
| HP Handheld Probe | 7.4 | 0.1 | 0.10 | 4.5 |

**How the models were meant to be read:**
- More fixations suggest longer search.
- A larger just-noticeable difference makes close temperature differences harder to judge by color alone.
- Higher visibility helps overview tasks such as zones.
- More controller actions per trial suggest more physical effort.

**Limitations:**
- The models ignore learning, fatigue, aesthetics and simulator sickness.
- They also ignore how people combine strategies, for example walking to a marker and reading it with the handheld probe.
