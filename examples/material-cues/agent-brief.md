# Study brief (given to every agent)

You are forecasting how a group of people performed and felt in a small virtual room shown at two rendering qualities. You have not seen their answers.

**The room.** A wooden table in a room with a plank floor, a brick back wall and plaster side walls holds nine everyday objects: two mugs, two bowls, two vases, a wooden box, a cushion and a plant. The two mugs, the two bowls and the two vases have the same shape and nearly the same colour; each material keeps its own slight tint. They differ in material: one mug is glossy ceramic and the other matte ceramic; one bowl is brushed metal and the other plastic; one vase is glass and the other plastic.

**The two designs.** People see the same room and objects in both.

- **Low:** flat, unlit colours. Every material is drawn in its average colour, without lighting, shading, textures, reflections, transparency or shadows, at reduced resolution and without antialiasing.
- **High:** physically based materials with full texture sets (colour, normal, roughness, metalness, ambient-occlusion and height maps). It adds surface displacement on the brick wall and floor, environment reflections, glass transmission, a clear-coat glaze on the glossy mug, brushed-metal anisotropy, fabric sheen, soft shadows, tone mapping and antialiasing.

**The participants and the procedure.** 16 adults took part. They wore a standalone headset and pointed with a controller ray; the same WebXR page also runs in a desktop browser. Each person tried both designs: 8 people in the order low → high and 8 in the order high → low, with a short break between the designs. In each design they:

1. did one practice trial: "Select the wooden box";
2. did four search trials: "Select the glossy ceramic mug, the brushed-metal bowl and the glass vase". Before every trial the six look-alikes swap places. A selected object flashes green when it is a target and red when it is not; a trial ends when all three targets are selected. The experimenter stops a trial at 120 s;
3. did six identification trials: a yellow ring on the table and a small yellow cone above it mark one of the six look-alikes (each is marked once, in a random order) and the person names its material from five options: glossy ceramic, matte ceramic, brushed metal, plastic or glass. There is no feedback on these answers;
4. answered a short questionnaire about that design.

At the end of the session they said which of the two designs they preferred.

**Information about the participants.** For each person the age band, vision correction (none, glasses or contact lenses), colour vision (normal or deficient, from a screening plate test), art or design training (none, some or professional) and total hours of VR use before the study were recorded.

**What to predict for each design.**

| Outcome | How it is measured |
| --- | --- |
| Search time | Seconds per search trial, from its start until the third target is selected (wrong selections included); geometric mean over the four search trials |
| Wrong selections | Number of non-target objects selected in the four search trials together |
| Material identification accuracy | Share of the six identification trials answered correctly (0–1) |
| Presence | Mean of four 1–7 items: general, spatial, involvement, realness |
| Visual appeal | "How visually appealing was the room?" (1–7) |
| Preference | Rank, 1 = the preferred design, 2 = the other |

Report each value for low and high, plus your uncertainty and reasoning.
