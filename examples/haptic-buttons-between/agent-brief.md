# Study brief (given to every agent)

You are forecasting how groups of people performed and felt when they typed codes on a keypad floating in mid-air in virtual reality, with one of three kinds of button feedback. You have not seen their answers.

**The setup.** Participants wear a Meta Quest 3 and use hand tracking; there are no controllers. A numeric keypad floats at chest height, about 40 cm in front of them, tilted slightly towards them. It has twelve keys in a 3 × 4 grid (1–9, then Delete, 0 and Enter), each a 4 cm square with 1 cm gaps. A key is pressed by pushing the index fingertip through its surface: it moves in with the finger and registers at 1.5 cm depth. The room around the keypad is plain and evenly lit, and there is no sound in any design. Every participant wears a small vibrotactile actuator ring on the index finger of the dominant hand, in every design, so the equipment is the same for all; it stays off with visual feedback only.

**The task.** A panel above the keypad shows a 4-digit code. The participant types it and presses Enter; the next code appears straight away. A wrong digit is removed with Delete and typed again. The task gives no time limit and shows no score.

**The three feedback designs** (the keypad, the room and the codes stay the same):

- **Visual feedback only:** a key lights up when the fingertip touches it and turns a darker shade when it registers.
- **Vibrotactile buzz:** the same visual feedback, plus one short vibration (20 ms) of the ring when a key registers.
- **Patterned pulse:** the same visual feedback, plus a pattern on the ring: a faint tick when the fingertip touches a key, a stronger double pulse when it registers, and a faint tick when the finger leaves the key.

**The feedback in detail.** The ring sits on the outermost segment of the index finger, just behind the fingertip, and holds a coin-type linear resonant actuator, 8 mm across, against the finger pad. It vibrates at its resonant frequency of 190 Hz; full drive gives a peak acceleration of about 1.2 g at the skin, and each burst reaches full amplitude and is braked to a stop within about 4 ms. A vibration starts about 20 ms after the tracking frame that triggers it. The visual feedback is the same in every design and appears on the next displayed frame: a key at rest is mid-grey, turns light blue on touch and moves in with the fingertip, stops at 1.5 cm depth and turns dark blue when it registers, and springs back to grey when the fingertip leaves it. The buzz is one 20 ms burst at full drive at registration. In the patterned pulse, each faint tick is a 10 ms burst at 35% of full drive, and the double pulse at registration is two 15 ms bursts at full drive with a 25 ms pause between them.

**Who took part, and the procedure.** 36 adults took part, between subjects: each person used **one** design only, and nobody saw the others. They were assigned in order of arrival in blocks of three, each block covering every design once, so 12 people used each design. Each person:

1. answered a short background questionnaire, put on the headset and the ring, and calibrated hand tracking;
2. typed two practice codes (not scored);
3. typed twelve scored codes;
4. answered a short questionnaire about the codes they had just typed.

**What to predict for each design** (the mean of the people who used it):

| Outcome | How it is measured |
| --- | --- |
| Code entry time | Seconds from a code appearing to the Enter press, geometric mean over the twelve scored codes (correcting wrong digits is part of the time) |
| Error rate | Wrong digit presses (a wrong key, or one key registered twice) / all digit presses in the scored codes; Delete and Enter are not counted |
| Presence | "The keypad felt as if it were really there in front of me", 1 (not at all) to 7 (completely) |
| Perceived workload | Raw NASA-TLX: the unweighted mean of six items, each 0–100 in steps of 5: mental demand, physical demand, temporal demand, performance (0 = perfect, 100 = failure), effort, frustration |
| Comfort | "How comfortable was typing on the keypad?", 1 (very uncomfortable) to 7 (very comfortable) |

Report each value for every design, with your uncertainty and reasoning.
