# Toy evidence note (invented for this example)

These statements are invented for demonstration. They are not a literature review, and they were not fitted to the synthetic answers. In a real study, put your evidence document here, for example a summary of published findings or a computational model.

- Visual-fidelity cues such as shading, specular highlights and reflections help observers tell materials apart, for example glossy from matte or metal from plastic.
- When materials can only be told apart by appearance, flat rendering is expected to slow search and increase wrong selections.
- Ratings of how realistic a scene looks usually react more strongly to rendering quality than presence does.
- Visual discomfort depends on more than image quality. Blur and aliasing at low resolution can increase it, and expensive effects can increase it when they lower frame rate.
- Preference rankings tend to follow visual appeal, but individuals differ.
